C2  REPORT_v0 Finding 2: 'tok/char agrees, which confirms the per-word number'
==============================================================================
tok/word and tok/char share the SAME numerator. Their ratio-of-ratios is
an algebraic identity that contains no token counts at all:

   (T_h/C_h) / (T_e/C_e)      C_e/W_e      chars-per-word(eng)
   --------------------- =  ---------  =  -------------------
   (T_h/W_h) / (T_e/W_e)      C_h/W_h      chars-per-word(hin)

  eng: tokens=   99  words=  78  codepoints=  448   chars/word=5.744
  hin: tokens=  459  words=  61  codepoints=  290   chars/word=4.754

  tok/word ratio (hin:eng) = 5.928x
  tok/char ratio (hin:eng) = 7.162x   (REPORT_v0 quotes ~7.0x)
  r_char / r_word          = 1.2081
  cpw(eng) / cpw(hin)      = 1.2081   <-- identical, by construction

The 19% gap between 5.89x and 7.0x is neither corroboration nor noise:
it is exactly the statement that Hindi packs fewer codepoints per
whitespace word than English. It carries ZERO extra information about
the tokenizer.
VERDICT: Finding 2 is not evidence. The two metrics cannot disagree except
by the chars-per-word ratio, so 'they agree' is unfalsifiable.
